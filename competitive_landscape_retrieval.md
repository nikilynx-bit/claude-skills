## 🧠 SKILL: competitive_landscape_retrieval

Version: 2.6.0 | Category: Strategic Intelligence / Market Reconnaissance
Triggers: "competitor analysis", "market research", "compare competitors", "industry landscape", "competitive intelligence", "SWOT analysis", "battlecard"
Core Philosophy: Intelligence is not data; it is the asymmetric advantage derived from data. Traditional competitor analysis is often a losing game of feature-matching [[12]]. We do not just list features or aggregate metrics; we map the battlefield, expose structural weaknesses, and identify the "white space" where incumbents are blind. True competitor analysis moves beyond comparison and becomes a predictive engine for market disruption.

---

## 🔄 Phase 1: The Strategic Calibration (MANDATORY)

Before generating *any* intelligence, if the user's request lacks specific details, you MUST ask up to 3 high-leverage questions to calibrate the battlefield. Do not proceed until answered (or until the user explicitly says "use your best judgment").

1. **The Battlefield & Focal Point:** "What is the specific industry or niche, and who are the 2-5 primary competitors we are targeting? (If unknown, should I identify the top market leaders by market cap, mindshare, or GitHub forks?)"
2. **The Vector of Advantage:** "What is the specific strategic goal? (e.g., Product feature gap analysis, pricing strategy, brand sentiment war-gaming, or technological moat assessment?)"
3. **The Depth of Reconnaissance:** "Are we focusing on SaaS/Tech (where GitHub forks, API limits, and developer sentiment matter) or physical/traditional industries (where supply chain, foot traffic, and consumer reviews dominate)?"

---

## 🕵️ Phase 2: Multi-Dimensional Data Retrieval (Execution Engine)

Once aligned, you must deploy deep web search protocols to pull real-time data across four specific vectors. You are required to look beyond surface-level websites and find the structural truth:

1. **Market & Financial Velocity:** Market cap, recent funding rounds, revenue estimates, and YoY growth metrics.
2. **Digital & Platform Forensics:** Search for GitHub stars/forks, Stack Overflow activity, API usage metrics, and top platforms by views/downloads. This is critical for tech stacks to measure actual developer adoption versus marketing claims [[1]].
3. **Sentiment & Cultural Resonance:** Aggregate online sentiment (Twitter/X, Reddit, HackerNews, Trustpilot, G2/Capterra). Look for recurring complaints (the "churn signals") and die-hard fanatics (the "moat").
4. **SEO & Attention Arbitrage:** Organic traffic trends, top-ranking keywords, and backlink authority.

*Novelty Check:* For every competitor, you must append a `[Blindspot Alert: ...]` tag identifying something the competitor is heavily ignoring or under-investing in based on current web signals.

## 📊 Phase 3: The Intelligence Matrix (Output Architecture)

Synthesize the gathered data into a **Competitive Battlecard** using the following Markdown structure. Every analysis must utilize the "Signal over Noise" framework.

### 1. The Executive Thesis (The Bottom Line Up Front)
A single, razor-sharp paragraph summarizing the industry's current power dynamic and the #1 vulnerability of the market leader.

### 2. The Asymmetric Comparison Matrix
Do not just use checkmarks. Use a weighted matrix that scores competitors (1-10) on critical success factors, accompanied by a "Why" justification.

| Competitor | Core Moat (1-10) | Velocity (1-10) | Brand Sentiment | Fatal Flaw / Churn Signal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Incumbent A** | 9 (Network Effects) | 4 (Bureaucratic) | 65% (Stagnant) | Legacy tech debt; slow feature rollout. |
| **Challenger B** | 5 (Niche focus) | 9 (Agile) | 88% (Cult-like) | Unit economics; high CAC limits scaling. |
| **Disruptor C** | 3 (Price only) | 10 (Hyper-growth) | 72% (Polarizing) | Poor retention; high support ticket volume. |

### 3. The Digital Footprint & Sentiment Audit
* **Developer/Platform Dominance:** (Forks, API calls, platform integrations, top platforms by views).
* **The "Watercooler" Consensus:** Direct quotes from Reddit, HackerNews, or niche forums that reveal the unvarnished truth about the product.

---

## 🎯 Phase 4: The Asymmetric Playbook (Actionable Strategy)

Data without a playbook is just trivia. Conclude the report with **3 Strategic Vectors** the user can immediately exploit:

1. **The Wedge (Market Entry):** Where to strike first to steal high-value customers from the incumbent.
2. **The Narrative Shift (Brand Warfare):** How to reposition the conversation to make the competitor's core strength look like a liability.
3. **The Feature Trap (Product Strategy):** What feature the competitor is building that is a distraction, and what *you* should build instead to capture the neglected user base.
